HOW TO Play

The grid is cut into coloured regions. Put exactly one cat in every row, every column and every region. No numbers, no counting — that is the entire rule.

Above is a solved 5×5. Every row has one cat, every column has one, every region has one. The pips along the top and the left edge tell you which columns and rows are already accounted for.

Tap a square to put a cat on it, and tap again to take it off. Hold a square to block it — a tap clears that too. Blocking what you have ruled out is the whole game on the bigger grids.

ONE CAT · GOOD TWO CATS · BROKEN

The move that cracks these: if a region sits entirely inside one row, that row's cat has to be in the region — so every other square in the row is out. It works the other way too, and once you see it you will see it everywhere.

Stuck on the very first move? On an untouched grid the hint button is free and fills nothing in — it dims everything the opening argument does not touch, outlines the row, column or region it rests on, and names the reason. You still make the move. Every hint after that reveals a square, and those cost.

AUTO X pencils a faint cross into every square your placed cats have already ruled out, so you can see what is left before committing. It is bookkeeping you could do by hand, and the button turns it off if you would rather.

A numbered tile counts the cats hiding in the eight tiles touching it, diagonals included. It never hides a cat itself.

Tap a tile to put a cat on it, and tap again to take it off. Hold a tile to block it — a tap clears that too. Blocking what you have ruled out is how the big grids fall.

CLUE UNSOLVED CLUE SATISFIED CLUE BROKEN

A clue goes lime when its count is right, pink when you have broken it. Every clue lime means the puzzle is solved.

Both games are built to fall to pure logic — you never have to guess in either. A hint finds a square you can prove, or catches a cat you have put in the wrong place.
